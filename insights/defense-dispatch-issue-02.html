Issue 02  ·  Week of September 28, 2026

This Week’s Lead

The Infantry Squad Vehicle Comes Into Focus

Two developments this month put the Infantry Squad Vehicle (ISV) — a platform with deep Fort Benning ties — at the center of Army ground modernization, and both carry a regional angle worth watching.

  • GM Defense’s $700M contract. The Army awarded GM Defense a $697.7 million contract modification (designated P00053) to re-engineer the ISV, bringing the total contract value to roughly $915.6 million. Managed by Army Contracting Command–Detroit Arsenal, the work modifies the five-seat ISV Utility variant — which trades rear seats for a cargo bed — to support logistics, fire support, command-and-control, electronic warfare, and counter-drone missions. GM Defense (Warren, Michigan) was the sole bidder; work runs through June 2028 (Defence Blog).
  • Why it matters here. The ISV program runs through the Program Acquisition Executive (PAE) Maneuver Ground and the Maneuver Future Capability Directorate (MFC) at Fort Benning — one of the programs we flagged in Issue 01 as drawing defense firms to the region. As the vehicle takes on new roles, expect openings for local integrators, sensor and EW suppliers, and sustainment providers.

Local Experimentation

The Battle Lab Puts Drones on the ISV

Even as the ISV’s design evolves, Fort Benning is experimenting with what it carries. In late July and early August, the Army’s Maneuver Battle Lab — with Strategic Spectrum Warfare and MFCD teams — experimented on two commercial tethered drones (tethered UAS, or Te-UAS) mounted on ISV-Utility vehicles at McKenna Field, Fort Benning.

A physical tether supplies continuous power and carries data between the drone and the vehicle, letting the aircraft stay aloft 50-plus hours (versus 20–60 minutes for battery drones) and bypass radio-frequency jamming by moving data over the wire instead of the air. The capability tested — persistent elevated ISR, radio relay, and target detection while the vehicle is on the move — is soldier-centered experimentation of exactly the kind that turns an operational need into a fielded capability. The Army is collecting soldier feedback before deciding whether the capability warrants a formal program of record (Army Recognition, Sept. 2).

Innovation Ecosystem

CMI2 and Catalyst Pathfinder at the Maneuver Warfighter Conference

Continuing a thread from Issue 01, the Civil-Military Innovation Institute (CMI2) brought its Fort Benning team to the 2026 Maneuver Warfighter Conference — themed “The Contested Edge” — this month to showcase how Soldier-driven ideas become battlefield capabilities.

CMI2’s local work runs through the Catalyst Pathfinder program, managed by the U.S. Army DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory within the U.S. Army Transformation and Training Command, in partnership with CMI2 and ACME General Corp. Pathfinder — which the team calls “Innovation in the Dirt®” — connects Soldiers directly with researchers and engineers to move field-identified problems toward tested, delivered solutions. Benjamin Hardy serves as the Pathfinder Warfighter Innovation Chief for Fort Benning; Soldiers featured at the conference included a 2026 Dragon’s Lair innovation-competition winner (CMI2).

Workforce

Columbus State and Fort Benning Launch a Military Spouse Career Accelerator

Fort Benning and Columbus State University have launched a Military Spouse Career Accelerator, aimed at helping military spouses build durable local careers. It is the latest step in a deepening higher-education–military relationship in the region, and a complement to the transition pipeline we covered in Issue 01 — roughly 2,000 Soldiers leave Fort Benning each year, and about 40% stay in the area. For employers, programs like this widen the pool of skilled, locally rooted talent (WRBL).

Regional Resource

Choose Columbus: A Site-Selection Hub for Defense-Sector Growth

For firms weighing a move or expansion near Fort Benning, Choose Columbus — the region’s economic-development organization — runs a site-selector hub worth bookmarking. It gathers shovel-ready sites and vacant buildings (including at Muscogee Technology Park), area demographic profiles, incentive packages, utilities data, and a major-employer directory — with defense, robotics, and aerospace named among its target industries.

  • Speed to market: streamlined permitting in roughly 45–60 days, excess permitted utilities, and a location about 90 minutes from Atlanta.
  • Workforce: a 250,000-plus regional labor pool, a Fort Benning veterans pipeline, and graduates from Columbus State University and Columbus Technical College.
  • Contact: Choose Columbus, 706-243-8980.

Site-selector hub: choosecolumbusga.com/site-selectors.

By Audience

What This Week Means for You

01 For Investors

  • The ISV as a franchise: GM Defense’s ISV revenue is projected to approach $700 million in 2026, with awards potentially exceeding $1 billion — a maturing platform with an expanding mission set (counter-UAS, EW) and a supplier ecosystem forming around it.
  • Counter-drone / tethered UAS: the Battle Lab test signals Army pull for jam-resistant, persistent ISR. Watch tethered-UAS and power/tether vendors.
  • Local innovation infrastructure: CMI2/Pathfinder and Columbus State continue to deepen the Columbus–Fort Benning ecosystem.

02 For Government & Community Leaders

  • The region’s experimentation base — the Maneuver Battle Lab, CMI2, and area universities — is producing visible national-level results. Making it easy for small firms to access that base compounds the return.
  • Workforce: back the spouse accelerator and the transition pipeline as economic development, not just support services.
