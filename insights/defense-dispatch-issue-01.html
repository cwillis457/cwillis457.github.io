Issue 01  ·  Week of September 21, 2026

This Week’s Lead

The SBIR Window Opens in Two Days

The Department of War’s SBIR/STTR Release 6 opens for proposals on September 23 and closes October 21 (DSIP topic listing). Its topics went into pre-release on September 2 and are listed on the DoW SBIR/STTR Innovation Portal (DSIP). It arrives after a long freeze: SBIR/STTR statutory authority expired on September 30, 2025, and Public Law 119-83, signed April 13, 2026, extends both programs through September 30, 2031, ending a lapse of more than six months (Congress.gov summary of S. 3971).

Act Now

Use the pre-release window. DSIP’s Topic Q&A is where proposers put questions to topic authors during pre-release, and Release 6 opens September 23. DoW topics are released on the first Wednesday of every month (SBIR.gov), so watch DSIP for the next release.

What small business owners should do now

  • Register early. SAM.gov registration and an active DSIP account are both required, and both can take time. Start this week, not the week of the deadline.
  • Read the full topic requirements on DSIP before committing proposal effort.
  • Build on user evidence. Reviewers reward solutions tied to a real problem.

What changed in the law

  • A new “strategic breakthrough” Phase II category applies to agencies with required SBIR spending above $100 million. Awards may reach $30 million over up to 48 months, but applicants must have a prior Phase II award and provide at least 100 percent matching funds.
  • Beginning in FY2027, each agency’s SBIR/STTR director must set a limit on how many proposals a small business may submit, on a fiscal-year, solicitation, or topic basis.
  • Expanded security and due-diligence review of applicants, covering cybersecurity practices, foreign ownership and financial ties, foreign affiliations, and listed entities. Expect real diligence on your company.
  • Agencies with SBIR/STTR funds left at the end of FY26 may use them for the program in FY27.

Source: Public Law 119-83 (GovInfo). The money is moving: Army FUZE recently made a $3.5 million Strategic Breakthrough award to FluxWorks (Army SBIR|STTR).

Local Spotlight

CMI2 and Fort Benning’s Innovation Pipeline

The Civil-Military Innovation Institute (CMI2) is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit focused on hands-on, warfighter-driven innovation. Its local presence is the Maneuver Innovation Lab at the Maneuver Battle Lab on Fort Benning, which began with a memorandum of understanding in May 2024 and held its ribbon-cutting on February 7, 2025.

Catalyst Pathfinder in action: the MRZR-Alpha 4

In a September 8 post republishing an Auburn University engineering college article, CMI2 described how its Fort Benning team, working through its partnership with the Army’s DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory, facilitated collaboration between Auburn researchers and the Maneuver Center of Excellence. With Catalyst Pathfinder support, Auburn’s Applied Research Institute and its electrical and computer engineering department are working under a $1.4 million contract from DEVCOM ARL’s Pathfinder Program. The project — the Mission Command Integration Module–XL — is a commander’s communications suite designed from the ground up for the Polaris MRZR-Alpha 4. It aims to unify separate radios, antennas, satellite links, and digital gear into one compact, durable system (Auburn Engineering original).

Regional Industry

Hanwha Defense USA’s $262M Army Contract

Hanwha Defense USA signed an Army contract worth up to $262 million to develop Mobile Tactical Cannon prototypes built on its K9 mobile howitzer platform — self-propelled artillery already produced in more than 3,000 units worldwide (WTVM). It is Hanwha Defense USA’s first major U.S. Army contract.

  • Six prototype systems are due in year one, with an option for 12 more.
  • Integration, testing, and work on vehicles two through six will run through a facility in Opelika, Alabama — about 30 miles from Columbus — alongside an engineering and program management center in Huntsville. The Opelika site follows a $2 million investment made in April and becomes fully operational this fall.
  • The company has said it plans to expand to multiple U.S. locations.

By Audience

What This Week Means for You

01 For Business Owners & Small Business

  • This week: SBIR/STTR Release 6 (above). For Army prize competitions that lead to Phase I proposals, check the xTech competitions page and the Army SBIR|STTR site for what is open.
  • The local market: Per Choose Columbus’s March 2026 analysis, Fort Benning generates about $4.75 billion a year for the local economy, and a recent Army decision gave the post and the Maneuver Center of Excellence new acquisition authorities. PEO Soldier, PEO Ground Combat Systems, and the Infantry Squad Vehicle program have already drawn interest from major defense firms seeking proximity to the installation.
  • Hiring: About 2,000 soldiers transition out of Fort Benning each year, and roughly 40% stay in the area. More than half use DoD SkillBridge, which lets employers host interns for up to 16 weeks while the soldiers remain on Army payroll.
  • Supply chain opening: Hanwha Defense USA’s new Opelika facility (see Regional Industry, above) will need local subcontractors and suppliers as it ramps toward full operation this fall.

02 For Investors

  • Reauthorization lowers policy risk. SBIR/STTR authority now runs through September 30, 2031, giving founders and funders a multi-year planning horizon.
  • Diligence is tightening. With the new security screening, cap tables and ownership structures get scrutiny. Founders who are ready for it are better bets.
  • A prime moving in next door. Hanwha Defense USA’s $262 million Army contract brings a major defense manufacturer’s U.S. production footprint to Opelika, roughly 30 miles from Columbus, with stated plans to expand further.
  • A local anchor to watch. The Army’s Janus Program will place three Radiant Industries microreactors at Fort Benning, with first operations targeted for 2028 (Army.mil). Suppliers in energy resilience, security, and integration around that project are worth tracking.

03 For Government & Community Leaders

  • Energy resilience. The Army selected Fort Benning as one of five Janus host installations, announced August 26. The commanding general framed it as a readiness issue: the post cannot go dark if the regional grid fails. That makes utility coordination, permitting, and workforce planning immediate topics for county and state partners.
  • Innovation ecosystem. Universities, technical colleges, the Innovation Lab, and nonprofits like CMI2 form the base. Leaders can help by making it easy for small firms to navigate SBIR registration, workforce programs, and local incentives.
